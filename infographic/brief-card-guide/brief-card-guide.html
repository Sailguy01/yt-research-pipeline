How to Read Your Video Brief

A field guide to the Telegram research brief — three reading speeds, every section decoded.
2 SECONDSHeader + Verdict → keep or skip
10 SECONDSWatchlist + Bottom Line → what & why
1 MINUTEVerify + Key Moments → dig in & check
HEADER LINE 🧭 Topic · duration · ⏱ transcript quality · Pivotal N/5 · Confidence H/M/L Pivotal = how many load-bearing ideas the video holds (brief length follows this, NOT video length). Confidence = how solid the facts are — it tells you how hard to run the VERIFY items.
TITLE + CHANNEL Linked title · channel · published time (CST) Title click = the source video. Times are always Central.
🎙 GUEST LINE Who is talking — name, affiliation, credibility tier T1–T4, conflict flags T1 institutional (heavy weight) · T2 established analyst (bias noted) · T3 unknown (narrative only) · T4 promotional (near zero). Identity comes from video metadata; unverified is labeled honestly.
🎯 VERDICT One bold sentence + a triage tag: ACT / WATCH / IGNORE Your skip-or-keep decision in plain language. ACT = changes the picture for a watched asset. WATCH = worth 30 seconds. IGNORE = routine.
📈 YOUR WATCHLIST → max 3 affected tickers, one-clause impact each Your money relevance. "→ none directly" = safe to skip for portfolio purposes.
BOTTOM LINE Plain bullets — the speaker's core claims, one idea each Length follows information density: thin video = few bullets, dense video = more. Never padded, never truncated.
✅ VERIFY Max 3 items, strongest corroboration first ✓ items were checked before sending (named source + date). Items without ✓ say exactly how to confirm — that's your one-click homework.
WHAT CHANGED Delta vs prior coverage — omitted when nothing changed
🕐 KEY MOMENTS Timestamped links into the video (max 5) 🎯 marks the single most decision-relevant moment — start there. Timestamps are honest: ±5s for auto-captions, ±10s for ASR, exact only for creator captions.
FOOTER Regime — speaker vs analyst · Unverified count · not-advice footer What the speaker claims and what the analyst infers are always kept separate. The unverified count is honest gaps, not hidden ones.

✓ The trust marks

  • ✓ = verified against a named, dated source before the brief was sent
  • no mark = speaker's claim — opinion, forecast, or unverified (base rate)
  • Unverified: N in the footer = count of claims the pipeline could not check
  • Regime lines never merge what the speaker said with what the analyst infers

🎙 Credibility tiers

  • T1 — verifiable institutional record → weight heavily
  • T2 — established analyst → informed opinion, bias noted
  • T3 — unknown → narrative only, never analysis
  • T4 — promotional/conflict → weight near zero
  • Conflicts are stated as facts, not editorial labels

Reading speed by triage tag

ACTread fully — watchlist impact
WATCH30 seconds — verdict + bullets
IGNOREskim the verdict, move on
Pair the tag with Pivotal + Confidence: Pivotal 4–5 = read fully regardless of tag. Confidence L = run the VERIFY items yourself before relying on numbers. Transcript source sets timestamp precision: creator captions exact, auto-captions ±5s, ASR ±10s.
The one rule that never changes: brief length follows the number of pivotal ideas, never video length — a 2-hour video with one key point gets a few bullets, a 20-minute video dense with claims gets the full card. And every brief ends the same way: scenarios are analytical, not personalized investment advice.
