# How to Read Your Video Brief — Field Guide

## Overview
This infographic is a reader's legend for the automated Telegram research brief. It decodes every section of the card, explains what each part is for, and shows the 2-second / 10-second / 1-minute reading path so anyone can extract maximum value in minimum time.

## Learning Objectives
The viewer will understand:
1. The three-speed reading path: what to read in 2 seconds, 10 seconds, and 1 minute
2. What every section of the card does (Header, Guest line, Verdict, Watchlist, Bottom Line, Verify, Key Moments, Footer)
3. How the provenance system works (✓ marks, [Unverified] counts, base-rate rule)
4. How to use Pivotal, Confidence, and the ACT/WATCH/IGNORE tags to triage

## Section 1: The 2-Second Read — Header + Verdict

**Key Concept**: The header line and verdict are all you need to decide whether to keep reading.

**Content**:
- Header: topic tag, duration, transcript quality (⏱±5s auto-captions), Pivotal N/5, Confidence H/M/L
- Title links to the video; channel + published time in CST
- Guest line: name, affiliation, credibility tier T1–T4, conflict flags
- VERDICT: one bold sentence + triage tag — ACT, WATCH, or IGNORE

**Visual Element**:
- Type: annotated card mockup with callout arrows
- Subject: the top of a real brief card (header, title, guest line, verdict) with labeled pointers
- Treatment: bright callout labels on the left, card content on the right

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "The 2-Second Read"
- Labels: "Topic + duration", "Pivotal 1–5 = information density", "Confidence = how solid the facts are", "T1–T4 = who's talking and why to trust them", "VERDICT = your skip/keep decision"

## Section 2: The 10-Second Read — Watchlist + Bottom Line

**Key Concept**: Your portfolio relevance and the speaker's core claims.

**Content**:
- 📈 YOUR WATCHLIST: max 3 affected tickers, each with a one-clause impact read. "none directly" = safe to skip for portfolio purposes
- BOTTOM LINE: plain-language bullets — the speaker's claims, one idea per bullet. ✓ = externally verified fact with named source; unmarked = speaker's claim (base rate)

**Visual Element**:
- Type: two stacked blocks with icons (target/bullseye for watchlist, list for bottom line)
- Subject: watchlist arrows pointing at ticker chips; bottom-line bullets with one ✓ example
- Treatment: the ✓ marked green; unmarked bullets neutral

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "The 10-Second Read"
- Labels: "→ = your money relevance", "✓ = verified by a named source", "no mark = speaker's claim (opinion, forecast, or unverified)"

## Section 3: The 1-Minute Read — Verify + Key Moments + Footer

**Key Concept**: For briefs that pass triage, this is where you dig in and check the load-bearing claims.

**Content**:
- ✅ VERIFY (max 3): strongest corroboration first — ✓ items with named sources and dates; others say exactly how to confirm
- 🕐 KEY MOMENTS (max 5): timestamped links into the video; 🎯 marks the single most decision-relevant moment
- Footer: Regime (speaker vs analyst, never merged) · Unverified count · not-advice footer

**Visual Element**:
- Type: split panel with checkmark column and timeline
- Subject: verify items as a checklist; key moments as a video timeline with a 🎯 pin
- Treatment: timeline bar with timestamp chips

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "The 1-Minute Read"
- Labels: "✓ + source = already checked for you", "how-to-confirm = your homework, 1 click", "🎯 = start here in the video", "Regime: speaker said it ≠ analyst agrees"

## Section 4: The Triage Cheat-Sheet

**Key Concept**: How to act on Pivotal, Confidence, and the Verdict tag together.

**Content**:
- Pivotal 1–5: how many load-bearing ideas the video contains — length of the brief follows this, not video length
- Confidence H/M/L: how solid the card's facts are (transcript quality + corroboration)
- ACT = changes the picture for a watched asset · WATCH = worth 30 seconds · IGNORE = routine
- Thin video = short card; dense video = full card — never padded, never truncated

**Visual Element**:
- Type: 3×3 mini-matrix or decision badge row
- Subject: ACT/WATCH/IGNORE badges; Pivotal dial; Confidence meter
- Treatment: bold color-coded badges (green ACT, amber WATCH, gray IGNORE)

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "Triage in One Glance"
- Labels: "Pivotal 4–5 = read fully", "Confidence L = run the VERIFY items yourself", "IGNORE = skim verdict, move on"

---

## Section 5: Provenance Rules (The Trust Layer)

**Key Concept**: What marks mean and what the pipeline never does.

**Content**:
- ✓ = verified against a named, dated source before the brief was sent
- [Unverified] items are counted in the footer — the brief tells you what it couldn't check
- Speaker claims are never blended with analyst inference; the Regime line separates them
- Every brief ends: "Scenarios are analytical, not personalized investment advice"
- Guests get a T1–T4 credibility tier with conflict flags; unverified guests are labeled honestly

**Visual Element**:
- Type: shield/checklist motif
- Subject: the four verification states (✓ verified, corroboration pending, unverified counted, speaker claim)
- Treatment: shield icon with checkmarks

**Text Labels**:
- Headline: "The Trust Layer"
- Labels: "✓ = checked before sending", "Unverified N = honest gaps, not hidden ones", "Speaker ≠ Analyst, always separate", "T1–T4 = guest credibility, conflicts flagged"

## Data Points (Verbatim)

### Section names as they appear in every brief
- "🎯 VERDICT:"
- "📈 YOUR WATCHLIST"
- "BOTTOM LINE (speaker's claims, unless marked ✓)"
- "✅ VERIFY (max 3, strongest first)"
- "WHAT CHANGED:"
- "🕐 KEY MOMENTS"
- "Regime — speaker: … | analyst: …"
- "Scenarios are analytical, not personalized investment advice."

### Tier definitions
- T1 — verifiable institutional track record; weight claims heavily
- T2 — established public analyst; informed opinion, bias noted
- T3 — unknown/unverifiable; narrative only, never analysis
- T4 — promotional/conflict; weight near zero, flag stated

## Design Instructions

### Style Preferences
- Clean, high-contrast for phone reading (the briefs are consumed on iPhone via Telegram)
- Color coding: green = verified/act, amber = watch, gray = ignore/skip

### Layout Preferences
- Reading order top-to-bottom must mirror the actual card order (header → verdict → watchlist → bottom line → verify → key moments → footer)
- Include a miniature annotated mockup of a real brief card as the spine of the design

### Other Requirements
- Language: English
- Portrait orientation for phone viewing
